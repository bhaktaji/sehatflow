## 2. Detailed Legal Findings & Distinctions

### 2.1 DPDP Act, 2023 & DPDP Rules, 2025 vs. IT Act Section 43A & SPDI Rules, 2011
1. **No Statutory "Sensitive Personal Data" Sub-Class in DPDP Act 2023, But Active SPDI Status Under IT Rules 2011 Until May 2027:**
   - The **DPDP Act, 2023** applies uniformly to all "digital personal data" without creating a separate statutory tier for "Sensitive Personal Data". However, **Section 8(5)** of the DPDP Act and **Rule 6 of the DPDP Rules, 2025** require Data Fiduciaries to implement reasonable security safeguards (encryption, obfuscation/masking, access control, visibility logs) commensurate with the nature and risk of the personal data.
   - Crucially, because MeitY's commencement notifications (`G.S.R. 846(E)`, dated 13 November 2025) defer the operationalization of substantive DPDP compliance obligations—and the repeal of **Section 43A of the IT Act, 2000** under Section 44(2) of the DPDP Act—until **13 May 2027**, the **SPDI Rules, 2011 remain legally in force today**. Under **Rule 3(iv) and Rule 3(v) of the SPDI Rules, 2011**, physical/mental health conditions and medical records/history are explicitly classified as **Sensitive Personal Data or Information (SPDI)**.
   - **Architectural Consequence:** SehatFlow treats all presenting complaints, prescriptions, diagnoses, clinical notes, and allergies as high-sensitivity health data / SPDI, enforcing strict role-based separation and field-level redaction.

2. **Purpose Limitation, Data Minimization, and Least Privilege (DPDP Act Sections 4, 6, 8 & SPDI Rules Rule 5):**
   - **Receptionist Role:** A receptionist's lawful operational purpose is front-desk queue management, appointment scheduling, prescription format/legibility verification, and assigning an appropriate doctor when a patient books without a prescription. A receptionist has **no clinical care purpose** to read doctor-authored clinical notes, diagnoses, treatment plans, or medical history timelines.
   - **Doctor Role:** A doctor's lawful clinical purpose is diagnosing and treating patients assigned to their care. A doctor requires access to the patient's `patientIdentifier`, presenting complaint, uploaded prescription, and chronological medical history, but does **not** require routine access to the patient's personal mobile phone number (`patientPhone` is redacted to `null` in all Doctor repository and registry responses).

### 2.2 Medical Record Retention: Statutory Mandate vs. Operational Visibility Window
1. **MCI 2002 Ethics Regulations (Regulation 1.3.1) — Indoor vs. Outpatient Distinction:**
   - **Regulation 1.3.1** of the *Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002* states: *"Every physician shall maintain the medical records pertaining to his / her **indoor patients** for a period of **3 years** from the date of commencement of the treatment in a standard proforma laid down by the Medical Council of India."*
   - While Regulation 1.3.1 specifically names **indoor (IPD) patients**, outpatient (OPD) clinics and doctors must also retain OPD consultation records for at least **3 years** (and longer where medico-legal claims, minor patients, or state rules apply) due to:
     - **Section 69 of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019** (2-year statutory limitation period for filing medical negligence complaints, with judicial discretion to condone delay beyond 2 years);
     - **Section 3.7.2 of the Telemedicine Practice Guidelines, 2020** (requiring RMPs to maintain consultation records, case history, and prescriptions);
     - **West Bengal Clinical Establishment Rules, 2017** (requiring clinical establishments to preserve electronic/physical medical records and registers).
2. **Separation of Routine Visibility Window (2 Years) vs. Storage Retention (>= 3 Years, Never Auto-Deleted):**
   - SehatFlow enforces a **2-year (730-day) routine historical access window** (`DataRetentionPolicyEngine.isWithinTwoYearHistoricalWindow`, calculated in `Asia/Kolkata` timezone) for Doctors and Receptionists viewing eligible completed visits/consultations.
   - Records older than 2 years (e.g., `cons_hist_beyond_2y_301` at 800 days old) are **automatically excluded from routine Doctor and Receptionist queries**, preventing indefinite routine exposure of older health data, **while remaining 100% intact in underlying storage** (`ConsultationHistoryRepository.verifyRecordRetainedInStorage`) and accessible to the owning Patient.
   - **No Automatic Deletion:** SehatFlow never automatically deletes or purges consultation records after 3 months or 2 years.

### 2.3 Patient Right to Access & 72-Hour Medical Record Supply Rule
1. **MCI 2002 Regulation 1.3.2 (72-Hour Turnaround for RMPs):**
   - Under **Regulation 1.3.2** of the *MCI 2002 Ethics Regulations*: *"If any request is made for medical records either by the patients / authorised attendant or legal authorities involved, the same may be duly acknowledged and documents shall be issued within the period of **72 hours**."*
2. **DPDP Act 2023 Section 11 (Right to Access Information):**
   - Gives Data Principals (patients) the right to obtain a summary of personal data being processed and the processing activities.
3. **SehatFlow Implementation (`MedicalRecordRequestTracker`):**
   - Patients have immediate self-service read access to their own consultation history in the app (`PatientConsultationHistoryScreen`).
   - Additionally, `MedicalRecordRequestTracker` tracks formal medical record copy requests with an explicit **72-hour SLA deadline** (`72 * 3600 * 1000 ms`) when directed to an RMP under Regulation 1.3.2, and a configurable clinic SLA under establishment workflows, with full audit logging via `ClinicalAuditLogger`.
